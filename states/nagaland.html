Nagaland's chain differs from most states: the lower tier is customary Village Councils and VDBs, not Gram Panchayats. Article 371A also protects customary law and resource practices.

How this catalogue works

The shared state template is kept, but the local-government logic is changed for Nagaland's constitutional and customary-law structure.

· The adapted pyramid

Nagaland's top layers are compact: 2 MPs, 12 cabinet members and 60 MLAs. The local layer is larger and customary: roughly 1,228 Village Councils plus VDBs and area/tribal councils.

Cards in every lens use the same rule: inline names · aggregate context · live register links.

Customary Local Governance · Village Councils, not Panchayats

Nagaland is a constitutional outlier: Article 371A protects Naga customary law and local customary institutions. The operational rural local unit is the Village Council / Village Development Board ecosystem, not a standard 73rd-Amendment Gram Panchayat chain.

Recommendation

Customary-law hybrid. A single HTML can credibly capture MPs, ministers, Assembly tally, district list and the constitutional exception. Village Council names, VDB records and area-council customary structures should be maintained as live registers because they are numerous, locally variable and legally distinct from ordinary Panchayati Raj.

Suggested next iterations

  1. Per-district pages · include DC/SP, sub-divisions, RD blocks, major towns and Village Council counts.
  2. ENPO tracker · maintain a dated chronology of statehood/autonomous-territory negotiations for eastern Nagaland.
  3. Customary institution schema · model Village Council, VDB, area council and tribal council separately instead of mapping them to Gram Panchayat fields.
