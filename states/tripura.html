Relmap - how the chain connects

Centre to State to District to PRI/TTAADC

Tripura's governance chain has the normal Union, State, district and regular Panchayati Raj layers, plus a Sixth Schedule autonomous district-council track for tribal areas. Citizens in the ADC area must be traced through district administration and TTAADC institutions.

How this catalogue works

A visual walkthrough of the Tripura-specific governance stack and the rule deciding what gets named inline versus linked to a live register.

Tripura has two local-government tracks

Regular PRI institutions cover the non-ADC area. The Sixth Schedule TTAADC covers most land area and uses autonomous district-council and village-committee institutions.

Inline names are used for small, stable rosters: MPs, ministers, House officers, High Court judges, district table and TTAADC top leadership. Large or volatile rosters link to official registers.

Local Self-Government - PRI plus Sixth Schedule TTAADC

Tripura is a two-track local-governance state: regular Panchayati Raj institutions operate outside the ADC area, while the TTAADC governs Sixth Schedule tribal areas through council and village-committee structures.

Sixth Schedule callout: the Tripura Tribal Areas Autonomous District Council (TTAADC) is not a normal PRI tier. It is an autonomous district council under the Sixth Schedule with legislative competence over specified tribal-area subjects including tribal land, forests, customs, village administration and allied local matters. It covers roughly 68-70% of Tripura's land area and about one-third of the population.

Scope memo - why this is one HTML page

Tripura is compact at the state/district layer but has two grassroots systems. The page therefore inlines the tractable leadership nodes and links out the high-cardinality PRI/TTAADC registers.
