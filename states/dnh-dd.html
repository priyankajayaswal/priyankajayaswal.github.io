Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu (UT)'s Governance Chain — at a Glance

A merged, President-administered Union Territory: no Chief Minister, no Council of Ministers, no Legislative Assembly, and three non-contiguous parts linked through an Administrator and district administrations.

2020 merger marker: Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu were separate Union Territories until the Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu (Merger of Union Territories) Act, 2019 took effect on 26-Jan-2020. Critics contested the merger as reducing local autonomy, so this page keeps the pre-merger identities visible in districts, Lok Sabha constituencies and local-body notes.

Relmap — how the chain connects

🔗 Union → Administrator → District → PRI / Municipal Ward

The chain is appointed at the top and locally elected at the bottom: Parliament represents the UT nationally, while the Administrator, Advisor and departments run executive administration.

Reading it: a resident has one Lok Sabha MP, a district administration, and either DNH-style panchayat representation or Daman/Diu municipal representation; there is no MLA or Chief Minister layer.

DNH & DD is not a state and not Delhi-style NCT: it has national parliamentary representation and local bodies, but executive power runs through the President-appointed Administrator and the UT Administration.

② The decision flow — inline or link?

Small enough to explain, too volatile to fully roster: Parliament seats, Administrator, Advisor, DIGP and Collectors are named inline. Panchayat and municipal ward-level rosters are linked because local-body seats and office-holders change through notifications, resignations and by-elections.

③ Identity lens

Why the page keeps the old names: the merged UT has one administration, but the map, MPs and districts still expose the three-part geography and the Portuguese Estado da Índia legacy in Daman and Diu. Keeping those names visible is part of the audit trail.
