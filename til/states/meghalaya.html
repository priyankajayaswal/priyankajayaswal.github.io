Relmap — how the chain connects

🔗 Centre → State → District → Autonomous District Council → Village

Meghalaya's local-government stack is shaped by the Sixth Schedule. KHADC, JHADC and GHADC are not advisory bodies: they are elected district councils with CEM-led executive committees and law-making powers over specified tribal-area subjects.

Reading it: a Meghalayan citizen has MLA/MP representation and, in most tribal areas, an MDC in a Sixth Schedule council instead of a standard ZP-Block-GP PRI chain.

① The governance pyramid

For Meghalaya the count jumps are small at the top (3 MPs, 60 MLAs, 12 ministers) and structurally different at the bottom: 3 Autonomous District Councils with 90 elected MDCs, CEMs and executive committees.

🏞 Local Self-Government — Sixth Schedule Autonomous District Councils

Meghalaya is the exception to the standard PRI story. In nearly the entire state, the local self-government lens is KHADC, JHADC and GHADC rather than Zilla Parishad → Block Panchayat → Gram Panchayat.

Sixth Schedule callout: the Autonomous District Councils can legislate on specified tribal-area subjects including land, forest (other than reserved forest), shifting cultivation, village administration, inheritance, marriage, social customs and traditional chiefs/headmen.
