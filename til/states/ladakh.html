Relationship map — UT without legislature

Ladakh's chain is shorter than a state chain. Parliament and the Union Home Ministry sit above the LG administration; LAHDC Leh and LAHDC Kargil provide district-level elected deliberation over development planning.

Reading it: there is no elected CM/Cabinet or MLA layer between citizen and district. Hill Councils are surfaced as the main elected deliberative layer.

The state template normally runs Centre → Cabinet → Legislature → District → PRI. Ladakh is different: the page routes Centre → LG administration → High Court/Districts → LAHDCs.

🏛 UT Executive — Lieutenant Governor Administration

Ladakh is administered as a Union Territory without a Legislative Assembly. Executive authority is exercised through the Lieutenant Governor and the UT administration, not through an elected Chief Minister or Council of Ministers.

No CM / Cabinet layer: the state-template Chief Minister, Deputy Chief Minister and Cabinet portfolio table is intentionally omitted. Ladakh has no Legislative Assembly and no Council of Ministers.

📍 District Administration — 2 current districts

Operational district count remains two: Leh and Kargil. A 2024 new-district process is under way; the official material found refers to five proposed new districts, not four sub-districts.

New-district reconciliation: the public record found for this snapshot refers to a New District Committee and formation of seven districts in Ladakh, with proposed additions commonly reported as Zanskar, Drass, Sham, Nubra and Changthang. The current official district portals and GeoJSON still resolve to Leh + Kargil, so the page count stays at 2 and flags the proposal separately.
