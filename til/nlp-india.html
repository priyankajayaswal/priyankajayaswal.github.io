What is the National Logistics Policy?

The National Logistics Policy (NLP) was launched by Prime Minister Narendra Modi on 17 September 2022 by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), Ministry of Commerce & Industry. It complements the PM GatiShakti National Master Plan (Oct 2021) and aims to make India's logistics globally competitive by lowering costs, improving efficiency and digitising the ecosystem.

Key Vision Statement

"To drive economic growth, business competitiveness and trade through an integrated, seamless, efficient, reliable, green, sustainable and cost-effective logistics network leveraging best-in-class technology, processes and skilled manpower." — DPIIT

Headline Targets

  • Reduce logistics cost to be globally comparable (current ~13–14% of GDP, target 8% by 2030).
  • Improve Logistics Performance Index (LPI) rank to top 25 (current: 38, up from 44 in 2018 — World Bank LPI 2023).
  • Build a data-driven decision-support mechanism (ULIP, LDB, LEADS).
  • Drive multimodal integration — rail, road, water, air, pipelines.

The Four Pillars & the CLAP Action Plan

NLP rests on four pillars and is operationalised via the Comprehensive Logistics Action Plan (CLAP) — eight inter-related action streams.

The 4 Pillars

1. Integrated Digital Logistics System (IDLS)
Single-window, paperless, API-driven. Cross-departmental data exchange via ULIP.
2. Standardisation of Physical Assets & Benchmarking
Standard pallets, containers, warehousing classifications, KPIs.
3. Logistics Human Resources Development & Capacity Building
Skill council, training, action plan for institutional capacity.
4. State Engagement
State Logistics Policies, City Logistics Plans, LEADS index ranking.

Flagship Digital Platforms

ULIP — Unified Logistics Interface Platform

A secure API gateway connecting 10+ ministries / 30+ systems & 100+ APIs to give private LSPs access to verified govt data (vehicle, vessel, cargo, port, rail). Hundreds of private logistics companies onboarded; transactions in the order of 100s of crores reported by PIB (Sep 2025).

LDB — Logistics Data Bank

RFID-based EXIM container tracking across India's major ports + ICDs. Tens of millions of containers tracked annually; widely cited as a model for transparency in port-hinterland movement.

LEADS — Logistics Ease Across Different States

Annual perception + objective survey ranking states/UTs on logistics. Categories: Achievers Fast Movers Aspirers. Drives state competition.

e-Handbook of Statistics on Indian Logistics

Consolidated statistics across modes, costs, infrastructure — released annually by DPIIT.

Multi-modal flagship projects status

  • EDFC (Eastern Dedicated Freight Corridor) — 1,337 km Sahnewal (Ludhiana) → Sonnagar (Jharkhand) commissioned. The Sonnagar–Dankuni stretch (~538 km) is being implemented under PPP, taking total EDFC to ~1,856 km.
  • WDFC (Western DFC) — JNPT to Dadri largely commissioned; freight density rising.
  • NW-1 (Ganga / Haldia–Varanasi) — Sahibganj Multi-modal Terminal commissioned Nov 2019; cargo movement scaled significantly.
  • Sagarmala — 800+ projects across port modernisation, connectivity and coastal community.
  • Bharatmala Pariyojana — Phase-I focused on national corridors / economic corridors / inter-corridor routes.

Impact on India's Existing Logistics Ecosystem

Macro shifts being catalysed

📉 Cost rationalisation

NCAER & DPIIT working group estimates put India's logistics cost at ~13–14% of GDP (older estimates were higher). NLP targets convergence to ~8% by 2030, releasing massive competitiveness for manufacturing/exports.

🚄 Modal mix rebalancing

Rail share of freight historically declined to ~27%. With DFCs, multi-modal logistics parks (MMLPs) and inland waterways (NWs), the policy targets a structural shift back toward rail + waterways for bulk cargo.

📡 Digital first

ULIP-led API economy is letting startups & LSPs build multi-modal visibility, dynamic routing, and ETA prediction on top of verified govt data.

🟢 Greener logistics

Coastal shipping + inland waterways + electrification of rail freight reduce per-tonne-km emissions; complemented by EV fleet incentives and FAME-II.

🏗️ Infrastructure unlocks

PM GatiShakti enables ministry-level infra de-confliction; warehousing modernisation, MMLPs at strategic nodes, port-rail-road integration.

👷 Skill & jobs

Sectoral skilling, formalisation of trucking and warehousing — opens up scaled, structured employment.

Why this matters for Eastern India

Eastern India contributes a relatively modest share of national GDP today, despite housing massive mineral, port, demographic and agricultural assets. NLP + DFC + Sagarmala + Inland Waterways converge here more than anywhere else in India — making the East the biggest beneficiary corridor of NLP-era infrastructure unlocks.

Challenges & Watch-outs

🏛️ Federal coordination

States vary widely in policy maturity (LEADS Achievers vs Aspirers). Convergence of GST, e-way bill, vehicle/road regulations is still uneven.

🚂 Last-mile & first-mile

Even with DFCs and ports, the rural & semi-urban road first-mile remains weak; cold chain and agri-logistics gaps persist.

💸 Capex intensity

MMLPs, port deepening, DFC last-stretch (Sonnagar–Dankuni PPP) — long-gestation capex with execution risk.

🛡️ Cyber & data

ULIP scale brings cyber resilience, data residency & access-control questions.

♻️ Green-transition

Trucking decarbonisation (LNG/EV) is nascent; rail electrification is largely done but freight EV ecosystem needs scale.

🧭 Skill gap

Demand for trained logistics professionals (warehousing, cold chain, port ops) outstrips formal supply.
