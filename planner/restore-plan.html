📄 Deep Tech Paper Appetite

Read papers like an engineer, not like you are being judged

Feeling dumb is usually a missing scaffold, not a verdict. The target is appetite: curiosity, repeated exposure, and small reproductions.

3 paper touches / week
Monday intake · 20 minScan arXiv, Papers with Code, company research blogs, conference proceedings, and trusted newsletters. Keep only 3 papers in this week's queue.
Pass 1 · 12 minRead title, abstract, figures, conclusion. Write: what problem, what claim, why it matters.
Pass 2 · 25 minTrace one figure or system diagram. Define all unknown terms in a scratch glossary.
Pass 3 · 45 minStudy one core section deeply. Ignore the rest without guilt. Depth beats fake completion.
Pass 4 · 60-120 minReproduce one tiny result, benchmark, proof step, toy kernel, eval, or diagram.
OutputA 10-line note: claim, method, limitations, what to steal, what to build next.

📘 Reviewing Courses

Turn course review into reusable engineering judgment

Do not merely re-watch. Each review block should extract a pattern, a checklist, a diagram, or a tiny implementation that can improve your real work and zenusers projects.

5 review tracks
Course 1 · Design System
Build taste, consistency, and UI leverage
  • Review one module every Tuesday or Saturday.
  • Extract components, tokens, spacing rules, states, accessibility notes.
  • Apply one lesson to a real page: restore plan, Study Hub, or a zenusers subdomain.
  • Output: one component note + one before/after screenshot or checklist.
Course 2 · Distributed Services
Build intuition for reliability, scale, and failure
  • Review one module every Thursday or Sunday.
  • Extract service boundary, data flow, failure mode, SLO, and observability lessons.
  • Map each lesson to office systems: latency, queues, retries, idempotency, backpressure.
  • Output: one architecture sketch + one production checklist item.
Track 3 · Daily Tech Reviewing
Stay sharp with current engineering movement
  • Review one credible technical source daily: release notes, engineering blog, paper thread, RFC, changelog, or architecture post.
  • Capture one signal: new capability, breaking change, emerging pattern, performance lesson, or tool worth testing.
  • Route it into the right bucket: office leverage, AI stack, zenusers idea, paper queue, or ignore.
  • Output: 3 bullets max. No doom-scrolling, no endless tabs.
Track 4 · PR + Design Doc Review
Train judgment on real engineering artifacts
  • Review one PR or design doc daily, preferably from your office context or a strong open-source project.
  • Look for correctness, missing tests, API boundaries, migration risk, rollout plan, observability, and operational failure modes.
  • Write one high-signal comment you would actually leave: specific, kind, grounded in behavior.
  • Output: one review note with risk, suggested change, and what you learned about taste.
Track 5 · Review My Love for Maths
Rebuild mathematical appetite through beauty and use
  • Spend 20-30 min on one elegant idea: linear algebra, probability, optimization, geometry, calculus, number theory, or discrete math.
  • Do one problem by hand before reading the solution. Let the struggle be part of the affection.
  • Connect it to AI, distributed systems, graphics, finance, physics, or an everyday intuition.
  • Output: one beautiful equation, one solved problem, or one plain-English explanation.
Review Method
Watch less, produce more
  • 10 min recap: what do I already know?
  • 35 min focused review: pause at every strong claim.
  • 15 min compression: one diagram, one checklist, one reusable rule.
  • 5 min routing: office, zenusers, or AI-stack application.

💼 Office Challenge

Work on what is important, not just what is loud

Every Monday, choose one challenge that increases trust. By Friday, ship evidence: a doc, prototype, metric movement, incident prevention, or unblocked team path.

weekly challenge selected Monday

Challenge selector

  1. Where is the team slow, fragile, or unclear?
  2. What would my manager be relieved to see solved?
  3. What customer pain has repeated twice?
  4. What system risk is everyone avoiding?

Execution loop

  1. Define one measurable outcome.
  2. Write a short proposal or decision note.
  3. Build the smallest proof.
  4. Share progress before being asked.

Friday evidence

  1. Demo, PR, architecture note, benchmark, or runbook.
  2. What changed?
  3. What did we learn?
  4. What is the next useful step?

🌱 Make It Stick

Tracking should create momentum, not homework

The system sticks when it is visible, forgiving, and useful in under two minutes. Track evidence of motion, protect recovery, and use misses as debugging data.

2 min daily · 20 min weekly
Daily scoreboard
Seven checkboxes, one visible next action
  • Use the top checklist as the only daily score.
  • Winning day: 4/7 anchors, not perfection.
  • Golden day: health + spirit + office + build.
  • Never add more than one new daily checkbox per month.
Weekly evidence log
One line per output
  • Health: workouts, sleep average, weight/energy note.
  • Deep tech: papers touched, AI pages, review tracks.
  • Office: decision, PR, doc, metric, unblock, or shipped work.
  • Build: demo, commit, page, prototype, or writeup.
Monday reset ritual
Admin day makes the plan runnable
  • Clear inboxes and calendar friction first.
  • Pick one office challenge and one personal artifact.
  • Choose papers, review tracks, and one hub system.
  • Pre-decide the minimum viable day for a busy week.
Fallback protocol
When the day breaks, shrink the plan
  • 10 min walk or mobility.
  • 5 min spiritual quiet.
  • 10 min office next-action cleanup.
  • 5 min learning breadcrumb or one flashcard.
Sustainability rules
Keep ambition metabolizable
  • Two intense learning days need one lighter consolidation day.
  • Do not punish a missed day with double workload.
  • Sleep debt overrides optional study, never health/spirit.
  • Projects must ship small before they grow large.
Review questions
Sunday asks, Monday adjusts
  • What gave energy? What drained it?
  • Which track produced reusable output?
  • What should be deleted, delayed, or delegated?
  • What is the one thing next week must protect?
