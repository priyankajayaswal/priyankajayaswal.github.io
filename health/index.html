🧬 Hormones: The Body's Chemical Messengers

Hormones are produced by the endocrine system and regulate virtually every process in your body — growth, metabolism, reproduction, mood, sleep, and stress. Understanding them is key to optimizing health for both men and women.

⚡ Sex Hormones
🔵
Testosterone
Primary male sex hormone (androgen)
Testes · Adrenal Glands · Ovaries (small)

The dominant androgen in males. Drives muscle growth, bone density, body hair, deep voice, libido, sperm production, and red blood cell production. Women also produce small amounts for libido, bone health, and mood.

  • ▸ Normal male range: 300–1000 ng/dL; peaks in late 20s
  • ▸ Drops ~1% per year after age 30
  • ▸ 20% population-wide decline in last 2 decades (endocrine disruptors + lifestyle)
  • ▸ BPA exposure → 50% lower T in adolescent boys
  • ▸ Phthalates → 20% lower T in adult men
  • ▸ Helps burn visceral fat — decline contributes to belly fat accumulation
  • ▸ Sleep deprivation, refined sugar, zinc/magnesium deficiency all lower T
  • ▸ Can be converted to estrogen via aromatase enzyme (especially in fat tissue)
♂ Primary ♀ Small amounts
🔴
Estrogen (Estradiol, Estrone, Estriol)
Primary female sex hormone
Ovaries · Fat tissue · Adrenal Glands · Testes (small)

Group of hormones governing the menstrual cycle, breast development, pregnancy, bone health, cardiovascular protection, mood, and skin elasticity. In men, small amounts regulate sperm maturation and libido.

  • ▸ Estradiol (E2): most potent form, primary in reproductive years
  • ▸ Estrone (E1): weaker form, dominant after menopause
  • ▸ Estriol (E3): produced by placenta during pregnancy
  • ▸ Directs fat storage to subcutaneous tissue (thighs, hips) rather than visceral
  • ▸ Decline at menopause → 8-10%/year visceral fat increase (SWAN study)
  • ▸ PFAS chemicals accelerate menopause by 1-2 years
  • ▸ BPA mimics estrogen — binds to estrogen receptors, disrupting signaling
  • ▸ Critical for bone density — rapid bone loss occurs after menopause
♀ Primary ♂ Small amounts
🟣
Progesterone
The pregnancy hormone
Ovaries (corpus luteum) · Placenta · Adrenal Glands

Prepares and maintains the uterine lining for pregnancy. Rises after ovulation each cycle. Also plays roles in brain function, mood regulation, sleep quality, and metabolism. Men produce small amounts for brain health.

  • ▸ Rises dramatically in 2nd half of menstrual cycle (luteal phase)
  • ▸ If no pregnancy → drops → triggers menstruation
  • ▸ Increases 10x during pregnancy (placenta takes over production)
  • ▸ Has calming/anxiolytic effects via GABA receptor modulation
  • ▸ Supports sleep quality — low progesterone linked to insomnia
  • ▸ Balances estrogen — deficiency can cause estrogen dominance symptoms
♀ Primary ♂ Trace amounts
🟢
DHEA (Dehydroepiandrosterone)
The precursor hormone
Adrenal Glands · Ovaries · Testes

The most abundant steroid hormone. Acts as a precursor — your body converts it into testosterone and estrogen as needed. Peaks around age 25, then declines steadily. Influences energy, immune function, mood, and aging.

  • ▸ Peaks at ~25, drops to 10-20% of peak by age 70
  • ▸ Precursor to both testosterone AND estrogen
  • ▸ Supports immune function, bone density, and mood
  • ▸ Decline associated with aging, fatigue, and weakened immunity
  • ▸ Chronic stress and high cortisol suppress DHEA production
♂♀ Both sexes
🎯
FSH & LH (Gonadotropins)
The reproductive controllers
Anterior Pituitary Gland

FSH (Follicle Stimulating Hormone) and LH (Luteinizing Hormone) are released by the pituitary and directly control the gonads. They orchestrate puberty, fertility, and the menstrual cycle.

  • ▸ In women: FSH stimulates egg follicle growth; LH triggers ovulation
  • ▸ In men: FSH stimulates sperm production; LH stimulates testosterone production
  • ▸ Regulated by GnRH from the hypothalamus (pulsatile release)
  • ▸ Excessive caloric deficit + exercise can suppress FSH/LH → amenorrhea in women
  • ▸ FSH rises at menopause as ovaries stop responding → used as diagnostic marker
♂♀ Both sexes
🍼
Prolactin
The milk production hormone
Anterior Pituitary Gland

Primarily stimulates breast milk production after childbirth. Also plays roles in immune regulation, metabolism, and reproductive function in both sexes.

  • ▸ Surges during pregnancy and breastfeeding
  • ▸ Inhibits GnRH → suppresses ovulation during breastfeeding (natural contraception)
  • ▸ Elevated levels in men can cause low libido and erectile dysfunction
  • ▸ Dopamine inhibits prolactin release (dopamine agonists treat hyperprolactinemia)
♀ Primary ♂ Low levels
🔥 Metabolic Hormones
💉
Insulin
The blood sugar regulator
Pancreas (Beta Cells)

Released when blood glucose rises after eating. Acts as a "taxi driver" moving glucose from blood into cells (liver, muscle, fat) for energy or storage. Critical for survival — but chronic excess leads to insulin resistance.

  • ▸ Visceral fat blocks insulin signaling → cells stop responding → insulin resistance
  • ▸ Insulin resistance → type 2 diabetes, energy crashes, cravings
  • ▸ Brain insulin resistance occurs in just 5 days of caloric excess
  • ▸ Exercise makes muscles responsive to glucose WITHOUT needing insulin
  • ▸ High insulin linked to PCOS in women
  • ▸ Fasting lowers insulin → activates fat burning and cellular repair
♂♀ Both sexes
🦋
Thyroid Hormones (T3 & T4)
The metabolism master switch
Thyroid Gland

Regulate metabolic rate, heart rate, body temperature, energy generation, growth, and development. Every cell in the body has thyroid receptors. T4 (thyroxine) is the inactive form; T3 (triiodothyronine) is the active form.

  • ▸ Hypothyroidism: fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance, depression, brain fog
  • ▸ Hyperthyroidism: weight loss, anxiety, rapid heart rate, tremors
  • ▸ Women are 5-8x more likely to have thyroid disorders than men
  • ▸ PFAS (forever chemicals) target the thyroid — disrupt hormone production
  • ▸ Iodine, selenium, and zinc are essential for thyroid function
  • ▸ Thyroid dysfunction can disrupt menstruation and fertility
♂♀ Both sexes ♀ Higher risk
⚠️
Cortisol
The stress hormone
Adrenal Cortex

Released in response to stress and low blood sugar. Mobilizes energy, suppresses inflammation acutely, and regulates the sleep-wake cycle (highest in morning). Chronic elevation is highly destructive.

  • ▸ Acute cortisol: beneficial — mobilizes energy, sharpens focus
  • ▸ Chronic cortisol: destructive — suppresses immunity, promotes visceral fat, disrupts sleep
  • ▸ Suppresses reproductive hormones (GnRH/FSH/LH axis) → lower T and estrogen
  • ▸ Amplifies visceral fat storage when combined with caloric excess
  • ▸ Follows circadian rhythm: peaks at ~8am, lowest at midnight
  • ▸ Cortisol and DHEA have opposing effects — chronic stress shifts ratio unfavorably
♂♀ Both sexes
🔄
Glucagon
Insulin's counterpart
Pancreas (Alpha Cells)

Opposes insulin — released when blood sugar drops. Signals the liver to break down glycogen into glucose and release it into the blood. Active during fasting and between meals.

  • ▸ Activates glycogenolysis (glycogen → glucose) in liver
  • ▸ Promotes gluconeogenesis (making new glucose from amino acids/fats)
  • ▸ Next-gen GLP-1 drugs target glucagon receptors for enhanced weight loss
  • ▸ Insulin and glucagon maintain blood sugar in narrow safe range
♂♀ Both sexes
🎛️
GLP-1 (Glucagon-Like Peptide 1)
The satiety signal
Intestinal L-cells

Released from the gut after eating. Stimulates insulin secretion, slows gastric emptying, and signals fullness to the brain. The target of Ozempic/Mounjaro drugs.

  • ▸ Natural GLP-1 is very short-lived (half-life ~2 min)
  • ▸ GLP-1 receptor agonists (semaglutide): engineered to last days/weeks
  • ▸ Fiber and protein meals produce more natural GLP-1
  • ▸ Intermittent fasting may naturally enhance GLP-1 sensitivity
♂♀ Both sexes
🍽️
Leptin & Ghrelin
Hunger & fullness regulators
Fat Cells (Leptin) · Stomach (Ghrelin)

Leptin ("I'm full"): produced by fat cells, signals the brain to stop eating. Ghrelin ("I'm hungry"): produced by the stomach, stimulates appetite. They work in opposition to regulate energy balance.

  • ▸ Sleep deprivation: ↑ ghrelin + ↓ leptin = increased hunger
  • ▸ Leptin resistance (in obesity): brain ignores fullness signals despite high leptin
  • ▸ Stopping GLP-1 drugs → ghrelin surges → "ferocious appetite rebound"
  • ▸ Exercise helps restore leptin sensitivity
♂♀ Both sexes
🌱 Growth, Sleep & Repair Hormones
📏
Growth Hormone (GH / HGH)
The repair & regeneration hormone
Anterior Pituitary Gland

Stimulates growth in children and maintains muscle mass, bone density, fat metabolism, and tissue repair in adults. Released primarily during deep sleep and after intense exercise.

  • ▸ ~75% of daily GH released during deep sleep (stages 3-4)
  • ▸ Declines ~14% per decade after age 30 ("somatopause")
  • ▸ Fasting significantly increases GH secretion (up to 5x)
  • ▸ High-intensity exercise is a potent GH stimulator
  • ▸ Supports fat burning, protein synthesis, and collagen production
  • ▸ Poor sleep → dramatically lower GH → impaired recovery
♂♀ Both sexes
🌙
Melatonin
The sleep cycle regulator
Pineal Gland

Released in darkness, suppressed by light. Regulates circadian rhythm (sleep-wake cycle). Also has antioxidant and immune-modulating properties. Declines with age.

  • ▸ Blue light (screens) suppresses melatonin production at night
  • ▸ Peaks around 2-3 AM in healthy individuals
  • ▸ ⚠️ Supplements are unregulated: found with 1,000-10,000x stated dose
  • ▸ Magnesium may be a safer alternative for improving sleep
  • ▸ Morning sunlight exposure helps calibrate melatonin timing
♂♀ Both sexes
💕
Oxytocin
The bonding hormone
Hypothalamus → Posterior Pituitary

Released during physical touch, social bonding, childbirth, and breastfeeding. Promotes trust, empathy, and social connection. Also plays roles in sexual arousal and pair bonding.

  • ▸ Surges during labor → stimulates uterine contractions
  • ▸ Triggers milk letdown reflex during breastfeeding
  • ▸ Released during physical touch, hugs, sex in both sexes
  • ▸ Reduces cortisol and stress — supports immune function
  • ▸ Social isolation → lower oxytocin → increased stress response
♂♀ Both sexes
🧠
BDNF (Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor)
The brain growth factor
Brain (Hippocampus) · Peripheral tissues

Not a classical hormone but a neurotrophin that acts like one. Promotes growth of new neurons, strengthens synapses, improves learning and memory. Critical for brain health across the lifespan.

  • ▸ Aerobic exercise is the most potent natural BDNF booster
  • ▸ Ketosis (via BHB) increases BDNF levels
  • ▸ Novel learning experiences stimulate BDNF release
  • ▸ Low BDNF linked to depression, Alzheimer's, cognitive decline
  • ▸ Omega-3 fatty acids support BDNF signaling
♂♀ Both sexes

🔗 Key Hormone Interactions

How Hormones Influence Each Other

Cortisol ↑ → Suppresses GnRH/FSH/LH → lowers testosterone & estrogen; suppresses DHEA; promotes visceral fat; impairs sleep (less GH)
Insulin ↑ (chronic) → Drives visceral fat → more insulin resistance (vicious cycle); in women linked to PCOS (excess androgens); suppresses SHBG → more free testosterone
Visceral Fat ↑ → Secretes TNF-alpha (inflammation); aromatase converts testosterone → estrogen in men; causes insulin resistance; activates immune system → fatigue
Sleep Deprivation → ↓ Testosterone, ↓ GH, ↓ Leptin, ↑ Ghrelin, ↑ Cortisol, ↑ Insulin resistance — disrupts nearly every hormone
Exercise (vigorous) → ↑ Testosterone, ↑ GH, ↑ BDNF, ↑ Insulin sensitivity, ↓ Cortisol (long-term), ↑ Leptin sensitivity — optimizes hormonal balance
Fasting (IF) → ↓ Insulin, ↑ GH (up to 5x), ↑ Ketones (BHB) → ↑ BDNF + GABA; activates autophagy/repair; ↑ Fat oxidation
Estrogen ↓ (menopause) → Fat storage shifts visceral; ↑ bone loss (osteoporosis risk); ↑ cardiovascular risk; mood changes; ↑ FSH (diagnostic marker)
BPA / Phthalates → BPA mimics estrogen + blocks androgens; Phthalates disrupt testosterone synthesis; both → reproductive harm, hormonal chaos
✅ Practical Hormonal Health Takeaways
🛏️
Prioritize Sleep (7-9 hrs)
  • ▸ GH peaks during deep sleep — poor sleep = poor recovery
  • ▸ Sleep deprivation tanks testosterone, raises cortisol & ghrelin
  • ▸ Stop eating 3 hrs before bed; magnesium (350-400mg) supports sleep
🏋️
Exercise Vigorously & Regularly
  • ▸ Boosts testosterone, GH, BDNF, and insulin sensitivity
  • ▸ Resistance training + aerobic: both needed for hormonal health
  • ▸ 10 min/day vigorous → 50% lower cardiovascular mortality
🥦
Manage Stress & Diet
  • ▸ Chronic cortisol crushes sex hormones and promotes visceral fat
  • ▸ Avoid refined sugar spikes — protect insulin sensitivity
  • ▸ Zinc, magnesium, vitamin D, omega-3 all support hormonal health
🚫
Minimize Endocrine Disruptors
  • ▸ Swap plastic containers for glass; avoid heating food in plastic
  • ▸ Ditch Teflon pans; use stainless steel or cast iron
  • ▸ Sulforaphane (broccoli sprouts) helps excrete BPA

💊 Dr. Patrick's Supplement Rankings

Dr. Rhonda Patrick's top supplement recommendations, ranked by priority. All data sourced exclusively from her Diary of a CEO interview.

🏆 Top 5 Supplements (Ranked)
#1
🐟
Fish Oil (Omega-3)
#1 Priority — EPA & DHA

90% of the US population is not getting enough; 80% globally. The only supplement shown to slow aging even in healthy, physically active people.

  • ▸ High omega-3 index = 5-year increased life expectancy
  • ▸ Low omega-3 is like smoking — equally bad for mortality
  • ▸ 66% lower chance of Alzheimer's with high omega-3 index
  • ▸ Slows epigenetic aging (Swiss study — even in physically active people)
  • ▸ EPA and DHA from fish oil are the best forms
  • ▸ Dose: 1.6–2g per day
  • ▸ Store in fridge/freezer (prone to oxidation)
  • ▸ Look for total oxidation value <10
  • ▸ Must be third-party tested, quality brand
#2
☀️
Vitamin D3
#2 Priority — The sunshine vitamin

D3 is the form made from sun exposure. D2 (plant form, mushrooms) is NOT as effective. Vegans should get D3 from lichen instead of D2.

  • ▸ Vitamin D deficient people who supplement: slowed biological aging by ~2 years
  • ▸ Doesn't help if you're already sufficient
  • ▸ People with darker skin need more sun (melanin = natural sunscreen)
  • ▸ D3 (not D2) is the effective form
  • ▸ Vegans: get D3 from lichen, not D2 from mushrooms
#3
💊
Multivitamin
#3 Priority — COSMOS study results

The COSMOS study (Centrum Silver in adults 65+) showed remarkable cognitive benefits. Contains cofactors for metabolism, neurotransmitter synthesis, immune system, and liver function.

  • ▸ Reversed brain aging by 2.1 years after 3 years of use
  • ▸ Reversed episodic memory aging by almost 5 years
  • ▸ Also slowed biological/epigenetic aging by a few months after 2 years (cumulative!)
  • ▸ Contains: Vitamin C, D3, E, K, niacin, B vitamins, folate, selenium
  • ▸ Soils are depleted (glyphosate depleting minerals) — we're not eating micronutrient-dense foods
⚠️ Gender-specific warnings:
• Men: Avoid iron in multivitamins (can cause oxidative stress, hemochromatosis risk)
• Pre-menopausal women: May need iron (16% are deficient due to menstruation)
• Post-menopausal women: Shift to men's needs (no iron)
#4
🧲
Magnesium
#4 Priority — 300 enzymes need it

Important for 300 different enzymes, DNA repair, cancer prevention, and sleep. 50% of the population doesn't get enough.

  • ▸ Important for 300 different enzymes in the body
  • ▸ Important for DNA repair — helps prevent cancer
  • ▸ Good for sleep (safer than melatonin supplements)
  • ▸ 50% of the population doesn't get enough
  • ▸ Need 350–400mg per day
  • ▸ Physical activity sweats it out — athletes need more
  • ▸ Sources: dark leafy greens, almonds
#5
💪
Creatine Monohydrate
#5 Priority — Muscles & brain

Creatine monohydrate is the most well-studied form. Benefits both muscles and the brain at different doses.

  • ▸ 5g/day for muscles (saturates in 3-4 weeks)
  • ▸ 10g/day for brain benefits (German studies)
  • ▸ 20-25g during sleep deprivation (negates cognitive decline)
  • ▸ No loading needed for general use (just takes 4 weeks to saturate)
  • ▸ Take with food/carbs to avoid bloating
  • ▸ Must exercise to get the muscle benefits
⚠️ Quality warnings:
• NSF certification is essential (contaminants including lead in processing)
• Gummies often have NO actual creatine in them

📈 Peak Span: A New Way to Think About Aging

A new concept from researchers at Duke University (and universities in China). Not just living longer, not just living disease-free — but maintaining within 90% of your peak function.

💡 What Is Peak Span?
📐
Three Ways to Measure Aging
From a Duke University pre-print
  • ▸ Lifespan = how long you live
  • ▸ Health span = living disease-free
  • ▸ Peak span = maintaining within 90% of your peak function
  • ▸ Even in health span, you're still declining — peak span asks: can you stay at 90%+ of your peak capacity?
📊 Peak Span by System

Different body systems peak at different ages and decline at different rates. Source: Duke University pre-print discussed in the transcript.

🔬 System-by-System Breakdown
🔴
Female Reproductive
Peak ~25, sharp decline to 40, then gone
  • ▸ Peaks around age 25
  • ▸ Sharply declines until 40, then effectively gone
  • ▸ Cannot maintain peak span without medical intervention
🛡️

Peak ~25 (or adolescence), steady decline
  • ▸ Peaks around 25 (or adolescence)
  • ▸ Then declines steadily to age 80
  • ▸ Sleep is critical for preventing immune aging
💪

Peak strength, muscle mass, bone density ~25
  • ▸ Peak strength, muscle mass, and bone density around 25
  • ▸ Then steady decline
  • ▸ Resistance training + protein is key to maintaining peak span
⚡

Processing speed, peaks ~25
  • ▸ Processing speed — "you can answer a question without prior knowledge"
  • ▸ Peaks around age 25
  • ▸ Declines after 25
📚

Peaks ~40-45!
  • ▸ "Library of facts accumulated over years" — pattern recognition, wisdom, problem-solving
  • ▸ Peaks around age 40-45
  • ▸ Why biologists and entrepreneurs do great work in midlife
❤️
Cardiorespiratory
Peaks 20-25, then declines
  • ▸ Peaks around age 20-25
  • ▸ Then declines over time
  • ▸ Exercise 5hrs/week + HIIT can reverse heart aging by 20 years
✅ How to Maintain Peak Span

These are "blanket things" that affect multiple systems — healthy lifestyles are multi-system targeting.

🏃
Exercise (#1 Thing)
Multi-system targeting
  • ▸ Aerobic exercise → increases BDNF (brain growth factor), grows new neurons
  • ▸ Resistance/strength training for musculoskeletal peak
  • ▸ Compound lifts (deadlifts, rows) — multi-joint, weight-bearing
  • ▸ Exercise 5hrs/week + some HIIT → reverse heart aging by 20 years
  • ▸ Even 10 min/day vigorous → 50% lower cardiovascular mortality
  • ▸ Urolithin A supplement helps improve cardiorespiratory on top of exercise
🛏️
Sleep
Critical for immune system
  • ▸ Very important for preventing immune aging
  • ▸ Prioritizing sleep is key
  • ▸ Also important for brain health
🐟
Omega-3
Brain aging
  • ▸ Really important for brain aging — supports BDNF signaling
  • ▸ Slows epigenetic aging even in healthy, active people
📖
Novel Learning
Brain plasticity
  • ▸ Engaging in novel cognitive experiences — discussions, new skills, new language
  • ▸ Increases BDNF and glutamate activation
  • ▸ Learning a new language → decreased Alzheimer's risk
  • ▸ Worst thing: retiring and watching TV → rapid cognitive decline
🥩
Protein Intake
Musculoskeletal maintenance
  • ▸ Important for muscle growth and preventing atrophy
  • ▸ Must combine with resistance training — both are needed
🚫
Avoid Endocrine Disruptors
Protect hormonal systems
  • ▸ Avoid black plastics (high BPA)
  • ▸ Testosterone decline → affects muscle mass and bone density

Key Insight

💡 Some organs (female reproductive) cannot maintain peak span without medical intervention. But cardiorespiratory, musculoskeletal, cognitive, and immune systems CAN stay close to peak with the right lifestyle interventions.
